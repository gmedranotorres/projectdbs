A khipu of a working life

For centuries, record keepers in the Andes stored numbers on khipus: cords of cotton or camelid fiber, knotted in base ten. I’m Gabriela Medrano Torres, a Peruvian fisheries engineer turned data and policy analyst, and keeping records people can use is my work. This page is my record. Select a cord to read it.

Khipu of Gabriela Medrano TorresDigital cords, 8 pendants. Peru, Spain and Chicago, 2018–2027. Knotted in the classic positional system: single knots for tens, hundreds and thousands; long knots for units; a figure-eight knot for one.
Single knot: one ten, hundred or thousand, depending on its height
Long knot: units from two to nine, counted by its turns
Figure-eight knot: a single unit
An empty stretch of cord is a zero.

Who kept this record

Fisheries engineer from Universidad Nacional Agraria La Molina, food-science researcher in Spain, business intelligence analyst in Peru, and now a graduate student in Computational Analysis and Public Policy at the University of Chicago, graduating June 2027.

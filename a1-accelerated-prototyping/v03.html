Questions and approach

What I want to work on next, and how every project gets done.

Questions that motivate me

  1. How can environmental monitoring data lead to stronger enforcement and better policy?
  2. Where are public services or community outreach failing to reach the people who need them?
  3. How can administrative records become evidence rather than disconnected files?
  4. When can machine learning improve targeting while keeping decisions transparent?
  5. How can maps make geographic inequality easier to see and address?
  6. How can food and aquatic-resource systems become more sustainable without losing sight of local livelihoods?

How I approach a project

1. Begin with the decisionWho needs the analysis, and what should they be able to do with it?
2. Examine the data honestlyMissing values, inconsistent definitions, duplicates, limits.
3. Build a traceable workflowTransformations someone else can follow.
4. Challenge the resultIs the interpretation supported by the evidence?
5. Deliver for the audienceModel, map, dashboard or plain explanation.

Teaching and languages

I teach programming to Spanish-speaking beginners, using block-based tools as a bridge to Python, and tutored K–8 students with UChicago’s Neighborhood Schools Program. I document my work in English and Spanish so the people who inherit it can maintain it.

# How to plan Gabriela's day

## Ranking work

1. Anything with no make-up that's coming up (Micro quizzes, the final, the Dec 1 fair). Protect study time in the days before.
2. Deadlines in the next 72 hours, nearest first. Count back from her real cutoff, not the official one: nothing after 21:00, so a 23:59 deadline means done by 21:00.
3. Weekly cycles that are graded every week: the clinic PR and progress comments (by Wednesday 21:00), and the clinic issues for next week (Thursday, after the mentor meeting).
4. Longer projects (Project v1 and v2, the Micro writing assignment). Give them a block most days, so they don't all land the night before.
5. Office hours: suggest one only when she's stuck on something that's due soon.

## Building the day

- Start from fixed events in `data/courses.json`, then lunch, then the 21:00 stop. Fill the gaps.
- Use 2 to 3 hour work blocks. Don't plan a block shorter than 45 minutes. Leave short gaps as "buffer."
- Give each block one clear outcome she can check off ("pdfplumber function returns text per page"), not a topic ("clinic").
- Add travel before anything in person, using the leave-by times in `context/commute.md`.
- On days with three or more fixed events, plan at most two work blocks.

## Moving to mornings (gradual)

She wakes around 9:00 and works best at night. She wants to wake at 7:30 and focus in the mornings.

- The current wake-up target is in `notes/state.md`. Move it 15 minutes earlier only after 3 check-ins at or before the target. Never move it earlier by more than 15 minutes at a time. Stop at 7:30.
- Morning block: start with lighter, well-defined work (setup, reading, a clear coding task). Keep the hardest thinking in a later block until mornings feel natural.
- Log the time she says she woke in `notes/log.md`. If she checks in late, start the plan from now. Say it once, neutrally ("Starting from 10:15"), with no comment on the target.

## Questions to ask (one or two, never more)

Ask only what changes the plan, for example:
- What she didn't finish yesterday, if the log doesn't say.
- A choice between two good orders ("Clinic PR first, or quiz notes first?").
- Something the data can't see (her energy, a new deadline, a group meeting).
